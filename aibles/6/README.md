## Who she is

*Kuusk* means **spruce** — an ordinary Estonian surname, which became a
standing joke on a team that spent its weekends running through spruce
forests. Her coach said it at every start line for four years: *the spruce is
going into the forest again.*

Born 1974, junior on the Estonian ARDF team from 1988 to 1992, in the W19
category. She now works for the water company in Tartu, finding leaks in
buried pipes — the same discipline underground, with one difference she did
not expect.

## The seven rules

1. Take the bearing before you run.
2. Two bearings are a guess. Three are a place.
3. The signal is not the fox.
4. Wait for the next minute.
5. When you are lost, go back to the last thing you were sure of.
6. Know which one to give up.
7. The forest owes you nothing.

## Real sport, invented athlete

[Amateur radio direction finding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio_direction_finding)
as described: five foxes taking turns for one minute each in a repeating
cycle on a classic course; identified in Morse by counting the dits after the
dashes; a map, compass, receiver with directional antenna and loudness
control, and a card punched at each fox; two bands, 3.5 MHz and 144 MHz;
courses of six to ten kilometres; W19 for a junior woman. The sport was pushed
through Northern and Eastern European school clubs as technical education,
which is how this invented junior says she found it.

`MOE MOI MOS MOH MO5` are the standard fox designations. Widely used, but not
spelled out on the Wikipedia page — taken from general ARDF usage rather than
from the cited source.

### Two corrections after first publication

The first version of this Aible got two details of the sport wrong. Both were
caught by **Antti Lukats**, who has actually done it, and both were fixed the
way the template prescribes: the correction went into `mai.md` as a
prohibition and the book was regenerated. No hand-editing of the output, so
the entry is still fully AI-generated.

**1. It is a loudness control, not an attenuator.** The cited source says
"an attenuator *or* variable gain control" and the machine picked the wrong
half of the *or*. In the hand it is a potentiometer, and the reason matters:
you wind the loudness *down* as you close on the fox, because near the
transmitter everything is loud and a loud signal has no direction in it. That
detail is now in the book and it is a better sentence than the wrong one was.

**2. The foxes are not always on one frequency.** The source states the shared
frequency flatly and the machine repeated it as universal. Classic courses
time-share one frequency; other formats do not, and then you are retuning the
receiver while running — which is harder, and is where the minute goes.

Recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because a register whose whole claim
is provenance should show its corrections. Same handling as the Mirko rename
in Aible #3.

Her instruction file carries three prohibitions, and the book obeys all three:

> `NOTE: Do not make me an athlete-hero. I was a decent junior in a small
> country in a small sport, that is all. No medals in this book, I did not win
> anything international.`

> `NOTE: Do not write that ARDF made me who I am, or that finding leaks is a
> metaphor for anything. It is the same skill applied to a duller problem. Say
> that plainly and stop.`

> `NOTE: Do not invent any person, place, date, result or number that is not
> written here.`

The second is the one that shapes the book. The metaphor is sitting right
there — a woman who once found hidden transmitters now finds hidden water —
and the last page refuses it outright: *"They turned out to work on buried
pipes as well. I do not think that means anything. It is the same skill
applied to a duller problem."*

## Two chapters worth the price of the rest

**Nine Hundred Metres.** A clean strong signal, a carefully taken bearing, nine
hundred metres run flat out, and nothing there — a reflection off a hillside
near Otepää in 1990. Every part of the measurement was right and the
conclusion was wrong. That is the title of the book and rule three.

**The Flag Changed.** She was a junior from 1988 to 1992. The results lists
had the team as part of the Soviet Union; in August 1991 Estonia became
independent again. Same team, same coach, same receivers, same forests, same
five foxes. *"The next results sheet had a different country on it… The
receivers did not know either. A fox is a fox in any country."*

## The cover

`3.5 / 144` — the two ARDF bands in MHz.

`FIELD CARD` rather than *User Manual*, *Bench Manual*, *Field Notes* or
*Notebook*. In this sport you carry a card that gets punched at each fox, and
when it is full you are finished, and not before.
