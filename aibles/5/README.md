## Who he is

Res Istor is thirteen, and lives in Quimper in Brittany. Swiss mother — *Res*
is a normal Swiss name, short for Andres. Breton father — and *istor* is
Breton for **story**, which his grandfather told him when he was eight. So the
name means *Res Story*, and he prefers that to what adults say when they read
it out.

Since he was nine he has written down every promise anybody made to him: who,
what, the date they said it, and later whether they did it. 214 promises. 61
not kept.

## The seven rules

1. Write it down.
2. Say the date.
3. Count yourself too.
4. A promise is not a plan.
5. Ask again once.
6. Almost nobody does it on purpose.
7. Keep the book when it is boring.


## The first Aible written from a child's age

The four before this one were written by adults with decades behind them. This
one tests whether the form needs a long life, and it turns out not to — it
needs rules and something that produced them.

What it does need is different handling. The rules are short and absolute, the
chapters are brief, and the reasoning stops where a thirteen-year-old's would.
His instruction file says so outright: *"One chapter each and keep them short.
I am 13, I do not write long."*

Two prohibitions in `res.md` do the heavy lifting:

> `NOTE: Do not make me sound clever or sad. I am not a strange boy, I just
> write things down. My friends know about it and they think it is funny.`

> `NOTE: Do not say I keep the notebook because of the roof man only. That was
> the first one, that is all I said.`

The second one blocks the obvious move — a wound that explains the whole
character. The book states the roof man as the first entry and stops there.

## The cover

`214 / 61` — promises recorded, promises not kept, on the day of writing. The
last page says they will be wrong soon and leaves them: *"a number with a date
is honest."*

`NOTEBOOK` rather than *User Manual*, *Bench Manual* or *Field Notes*. It is a
notebook.

## One number the machine computed

`res.md` gives 214 and 61. The book also says **153 were kept**, in the
chapter on rule seven. That figure is not in the source file — it is 214 − 61,
arithmetic on the author's own numbers rather than an invented fact, and a boy
tracking 214 promises would know it.

Recorded here because the instruction file said not to introduce numbers that
are not in it, and this is the one place the machine went past that. Judge it
as derived, not invented.

Two other departures, both corrections rather than additions: `res.md` wrote
*Loic* and *Glenan*; the book uses the correct French and Breton spellings,
**Loïc** and **Glénan**.
