## Why it is in the register

Because it is the largest thing of its kind that exists, and a register that
omits it would be a register of my own work rather than of a phenomenon.

Forty days and forty nights, for a book made in conversation with a machine. That
is not a neutral number and I do not believe it was an accident.

## He does not call it an Aible

*Aible* is my word, not his. Thierry Ehrmann has never used it, has not endorsed
this register, and is not associated with it in any way. He calls his book what
it says on the cover.

What I claim is narrower and it is a claim about my own taxonomy, not about his
work: by the definition used in this register — a Bible-like book made with the
help of an AI — his book qualifies, on a scale that none of mine approach. If he
would reject the label, the label is wrong and the entry stands as a pointer to
his book rather than as a classification of it.

## What method 4 is, and why this book defines it

Methods 1 to 3 in this register are all versions of the same shape: a prompt goes
in, a book comes out, and the human's contribution is measured in minutes.

Method 4 is not that. It is sustained dialogue, over weeks, where the human is
present for every exchange and the book is the transcript of the thinking rather
than the product of an instruction. The labour is not compressed — it is forty
days long.

That is a different category of artefact, and I did not have an example of it
until I found this one. Aibles 1 through 8 are experiments in how little human
input a book can be made from. This is the opposite experiment.
