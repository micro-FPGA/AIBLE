## Who she is

Kate Aray decapsulates dead chips, photographs the dies, and publishes the
images for nothing. Born Kadriye Aray in Izmir in 1990; shortened her own
name at seven because nobody at school in Rotterdam said it right; works out
of a garage that belongs to her sister in Eindhoven. 1847 dies in the public
archive on the day the book was written.

She does not repair anything. She keeps records for repairs that have not
happened yet.

## The seven rules

1. The die is the document.
2. Photograph it before you change it.
3. Publish it, or it did not survive.
4. Say what you cannot see.
5. Slow is a method, not a delay.
6. Somebody's grandmother built this.
7. Nothing a repair needs should be a secret.

## The cover

`ULA` — Uncommitted Logic Array, the old name for a gate array: the part
before anybody has decided what it is for. It gives the book its title and
she notes, drily, that it describes her too.

`1847` — dies in the archive on the day of writing. The book states outright
that the number is already wrong and asks to be left wrong, "because a
record with a date on it is honest and a record that claims to be current is
not."

`FIELD NOTES` rather than *User Manual* or *Bench Manual*. A field note is
what you write at the time, knowing it is incomplete.

## How it was made

Same two-step commission as Aible #3: the AI was told to invent a woman
called Kate Aray and fill in the blank template as if it were her, producing
`kate.md`; then it was handed `kate.md` and told to execute it.

Her instruction file carries her own prohibitions, and the book obeys them:

> `NOTE: Do not make me sad or noble. I am not grieving over chips, it is a
> job and I like it. The dead city thing is an observation, not a mood.`

> `NOTE: Do not write that my grandmother's ability caused my career. I do
> not know that. It is a thing I noticed afterwards.`

That second one matters. Her grandmother Hediye could name a failing loom
from the doorway of a hall with forty of them running, and Kate photographs
silicon — an obvious causal story that the book explicitly refuses:

> *I am not going to claim she made me what I am. I do not know that, and I
> only noticed the shape of it years afterwards. What I will say is what my
> job is, plainly: Hediye's job, with a written record attached.*

## Note on the name

`Kate Aray` reads as *gate array*. Unlike Mirko Controller in Aible #3, she
made the pun herself and by accident: she shortened Kadriye to Kate at seven
years old, and a man at a conference in Grenoble noticed it twenty-one years
later. *"I did the shortening. I did not see it coming. Both of those are
true."*
