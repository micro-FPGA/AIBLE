## Why this one is different from the rest

Aibles 2 to 8 invent a person and write their book. Aible 9 is not mine at all —
it is Thierry Ehrmann's own 1,800-page *Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI*,
republished under his licence because a register that omitted it would be a
register of my own work rather than of a phenomenon.

This one sits between the two. The **persona is invented**; the **facts are
not**. Everything factual came off the public internet on 22 September 2026 —
press releases, his own sites, Wikipedia, Wikidata — and the book carries a
Sources page naming every one of them with the date it was read. Inline
attribution runs through the chapters as well, in grey, under the claims.

It is the first entry in this register written about a **real living person**,
and that is the reason it is fenced more heavily than any of the others: a note
page at the front, a modified disclaimer, and a rule in the instruction file that
every sentence must survive being read by him.

## The five rules

```
1. Transmission, without creating a copy.
2. Connections.
3. Relations.
4. Love.
5. ?
```

His book has twenty-two rules. These are five, and they are not a summary,
a subset or a translation of those twenty-two. They are what came back from a
reading — which is the entire subject of rule one.

**Rule one** is earned by one flat sentence in the instruction file: 1,800 pages
were read, and not one of the 22 rules could be recalled afterwards. Nothing was
copied across, and five rules came out anyway.

**Rules two and three** arrived as single bare words with no story behind them.
The book says so on the page rather than inventing evidence, then reads each one
against a public fact and marks the reading as the machine's.

**Rule five is a question mark and is still a question mark on its own chapter's
last line.** It is not guessed at. A language model asked to complete a pattern
will complete it; that is the whole of what it does, and it is exactly why it
must not here.

## One correction after the first build

**The book was written with ChatGPT, not Gemini.** The first build of the *Forty
Days and Forty Nights* chapter said Gemini, and it was wrong.

The error came from the headline of the 1 September 2026 press release, which
reads *"un liber mundi, dixit Gemini"* — Gemini **said** "liber mundi" about the
book, as one reader among several. No release names the writing AI at all. The
machine took a name standing next to a book for a credit, which is precisely the
failure that rule 1 of section 10 of the template exists to prevent:

> Never invent a causal link between two facts standing next to each other.

Caught by **Antti Lukats**, who read the book. Fixed the way the template
prescribes — the correction went into `terry.md` as a prohibition and the
document was regenerated, no hand-editing of the output:

> `NOTE: the book was written with ChatGPT. Do NOT write that it was written with
> Gemini, and do not imply it.`

> `NOTE: "dixit Gemini" in the press release headline means Gemini SAID "liber
> mundi" about the book. Gemini is a commentator there, not the writer.`

The finished chapter now names ChatGPT as the writer, keeps *dixit Gemini* as
what it actually is, and says in its source line that the first build got it
wrong. Recorded here rather than quietly repaired, same as the two corrections
in Aible #6.

## Three unverified items, marked as such in the book

1. The ninety-nine words of 9 December 1999 and their five headings come from the
   instruction file only. No public page carrying them was found.
2. The exchange — *"Do you dream?" / "I dream of the questions you haven't asked
   yet."* — is attributed in the instruction file to a posting on X. The post was
   not located and verified. The book says so on the same page it quotes them.
3. That the writing was entrusted to **ChatGPT**. No press release names the
   writing AI; this rests on the register keeper having read the book.

Both are printed with their provenance attached rather than quietly upgraded to
fact.

## Where the sources disagree

The count of works at the Abode of Chaos differs between his own site (~6,300
works, 9,000 m², ~180,000 visitors a year) and the March 2025 ministerial release
(~7,200 works, 7,555 m², 2.5 million visitors since 2006). The book prints both
with their dates and does not average them or prefer the larger.
