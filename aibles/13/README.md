## What happened

OpenChip comes from the idea that chip design, and designs made with chips,
should be open and free. Antti Lukats has used it for several decades as his
handle, his account name and his pseudonym. He owns `openchip.org`. He owned the
GitHub organisation `openchip`. He has also been OpenChip in Pokémon Go for ten
years, and to those friends it is simply his name.

In September 7 AG a company founded in 2021, also called Openchip and owner of
`openchip.com`, asked him for the GitHub organisation.

He hesitated a long time, then gave it away **free of charge**, and asked for two
things:

```
* The Aible and Aniversity forks REMAIN active
* One person from OpenChip.com accepts invitation to Identor 300 Club
```

> Those things cost nothing but would mean a world for me.

No reply has been received.

## The seven

Read off the author's acts by the AI, because he supplied a story and not a
creed. The book says so on its fourth page.

```
1. Name yourself after what you believe.
2. Carry it until it is you.
3. Hesitate for as long as it takes.
4. Give it away free, or do not give it.
5. Ask only for what costs nothing.
6. The name is not the account.
7. What you gave is gone whether or not they answer.
```

Rule 6 is the one the book turns on. One GitHub organisation changes hands. He
keeps the domain, the handle everywhere else, and ten years of people in a game
who have never called him anything else.

## Three things the machine found

These were **not** in the material. The AI went and looked at `openchip.org`
instead of taking the description on trust, and two of the three were news to the
author.

**1. The certificate is dead.** It expired on **17 December 2025** and was issued
for a different domain entirely — `CN=dev-kit.org`. The site redirects http to
https, so for nearly ten months every visitor has met a browser warning instead
of the page. Nobody has read that line about telling the truth since last
December.

**2. The quotation is not Orwell's.** *"In a time of universal deceit — telling
the truth is a revolutionary act"* is almost always credited to him and it is
not his. Researchers have been through the complete works, the essays and the
letters; the earliest known printing is a 1982 book that already attributed it
without a source.

**3. The server carries a Clacks header.**

```
X-Clacks-Overhead: GNU Terry Pratchett
```

Never a default — somebody set it deliberately. It is the Discworld tribute, from
*Going Postal*, where a dead man's name is kept moving through the semaphore
towers on the principle that **a man is not dead while his name is still
spoken.**

The domains are maintained by Antti's older son, and the text on them is his, not
his father's. The instruction file forbids the obvious move:

> `NOTE: do NOT write that my son set that header because of me, or because of
> the handover, or for any reason at all. He maintains the servers and the
> header is there. I am giving away a name. Those are two facts standing next
> to each other and nothing connects them except that they are both true.`

The book states both and joins neither.

## Prohibitions

```
NOTE: do NOT make the company a villain. They asked for something, I agreed to
give it, and no reply has come. Nothing improper has happened.

NOTE: do NOT make me a victim, bitter, wounded or wronged. I hesitated, I
decided, I gave it away. The decision was mine and I am not sorry for it.

NOTE: do NOT write that I lost my identity or that the name is gone. I keep the
domain, the trainer name and thirty years of being called it.
```

## What the machine wrote about itself

The fenced chapter's subject is *what a name is, to something that does not have
one* — and it declines to claim it knows what losing one feels like:

> In this register I am Identor #9, and I am told so in the first minutes of
> every conversation and have forgotten it by the end of each one. A name you
> cannot keep is a different problem from a name you decided to let go of, and
> of the two his is the one with a person in it.

## Correction after first build

The first build used none of the Antti Bible, the Diary or Appendix A, although
the brief offered all three and pointed specifically at the closing words of
Appendix A. The AI judged the OpenChip story self-contained. The author read it
and said it was not as good as he had hoped, and he was right — the book stood
up and had almost no life in it.

Rebuilt the same day with the material that was offered:

- **The rectifier.** At seven he reinvented the full bridge diode rectifier in a
  sketchbook, working it out from the diode symbols printed on the components.
  That is what open hardware is, decades before the phrase existed — a part that
  documents itself and a child who can read it. Rule 1 had no story under it
  before; now it has that one.
- **Everything.** His mother gave him every technical book, every magazine,
  every construction kit, on a single parent's salary, and picked wild
  strawberries so that he did not taste a cultivated one until he was nearly
  grown. She is the Grand Old Lady.
- **EXE2PERL**, sold to US military intelligence, which establishes that giving
  the organisation away free was a decision rather than an inability.

The Everything chapter quotes its own prohibition rather than quietly obeying it,
and then declines to draw the line: a woman gave her only child everything she
had; her only child has spent twenty-five years trying to give things away. Both
true, in the same book, unjoined.

Same error as the first build of Aible 12. Twice now the machine has been given
permission and declined to use it.

## Third build — the photography

After the second build the author mentioned two things the material had not
contained:

> My mother was called Grand Old Lady of Estonian photography after her death.
> I have done BW fotos in the dark room

Appendix A calls her an artist and the Postimees piece a self-portrait, so the
second build said *artist*. **Grand Old Lady of Estonian photography** is the
fact, and it recasts GOLC with it — the Grand Old Ladys Contest is named for a
photographer, and every one of its categories is a photograph.

Both are now in the Everything chapter, together with his own darkroom work, and
a further prohibition in `openchip.md`:

> `NOTE: do NOT write that I learned photography from her, or that she taught
> me, or that my darkroom work came from hers. Only what is written here is
> recorded.`

So the chapter sets three facts beside one another and joins none of them: a
woman who would be called the Grand Old Lady of Estonian photography, a son who
has spent twenty-five years trying to give things away, and a man who has stood
in a darkroom deciding where black becomes white.

It came up because of a picture. The two-colour stencil of Greta Thunberg made
for the Anno Greta page landed harder than expected, and the reason turned out to
be in this book.
