cornflower is the national flower, the flower stalls at the Viru Gate are real

standing at them did.

The AI chapter must not claim consciousness and must not claim certainty that it

## 7. Rules

```
1. Always an odd number.
2. Say which one dies first.
3. Never ask what they are for.
4. Open at seven, also in January.
5. Grow the one nobody buys.
6. Learn the Latin. Use the other name.
7. The flower is not the gift. The carrying is.
```

One commentary chapter per rule. Each earned by a story below. Where a rule has
thin evidence, the chapter says so.

## 8. Stories

```
LINDA AT THE WALL
Learned the trade from a woman who stood at the Viru Gate stalls for forty-one
years. Linda taught by not explaining. Rule: 4.

THE TWELVE ROSES
A man wanted twelve roses for a birthday. Sold him eleven and told him why.
He was annoyed, then not. Rule: 1.

THE FRIDAY MAN
One white carnation, every Friday, fourteen months. Then he stopped coming.
Never learned who it was for or why it ended. Rule: 3.

MINUS TWENTY-ONE
February, shop heater failed overnight. Carried the tulips home inside her coat
on the number 3 tram. Lost the gerberas. Rule: 4.

JAANIPÄEV
Midsummer. Nobody buys cut flowers on the one night everyone is outdoors among
them. Makes wreaths instead, and closes early. Rule: 7.

THE CORNFLOWER
Rukkilill. National flower. Grows in every ditch in the country, which is why
nobody will pay for it. Keeps a row of it anyway. Rule: 5.

THREE EUROS
A girl came in with three euros for her mother. One gerbera, wrapped properly,
same paper as the fifty-euro bouquets. Rule: 7.

ELEVEN AND TWO
A wedding at eleven and a funeral at two, from the same bucket of white lilies.
The flowers were identical. Everything else was the number and the ribbon.
Rule: 1.

GRANDMOTHER, EVEN
Takes an even number to her grandmother's grave, deliberately, and it is the
only time the number is not a mistake. Rule: 1.

THE LATIN
Learned every Latin name. Uses them with suppliers and never with customers,
because a person who wants flowers is not asking for taxonomy. Rule: 6.
```
