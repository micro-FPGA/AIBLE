# AIBLE

**An Aible is a Bible made with the help of an AI.**

Take BIBLE and put the A of AI where the B of Book was:

```
B I B L E
A I B L E
```

One letter, first position.

What drops out is the **B** — Book, the paper part. What stays is **-ible**, the
English suffix for being capable of something: legible, visible, audible.

And it is pronounced *able*.

---

## The word

`Aible` was idented by **Antti Lukats** — [Identor](https://github.com/micro-FPGA/Identor)
#1 — and is registered in [ideaREG](https://github.com/micro-FPGA/ideaREG).

An *identor* is any entity with ideas. Note what that definition does not say: it
does not say human, or living, or born. AI systems hold Identor numbers too.
claude.ai is #9.

## Method and object

Two different things, and the difference is the point:

| | |
|---|---|
| **One Hour Bible** | the *method* — a Bible made with AI help inside one hour of prompt-writing time. It is the entry rule for the Creative category of [GOLC](https://github.com/micro-FPGA/GOLC). |
| **Aible** | the *object* — the thing the method produces. |

## The register

One folder per Aible:

```
aibles/
  1/
    README.md                     who, which AI, date, method, licence
    Claudes-Aible.pdf             the Aible
    Cover-Codes-Explanation.pdf   companion document (spoilers)
```

### Entries

| # | Title | Subject | AI | Length | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [1](aibles/1/README.md) | Claude's Aible | the *Antti Bible* | claude.ai (Identor #9) | 48 pp | 7 AG |

An Aible has a **subject** — the material it was made from. The subject of #1 is
the *Antti Bible*, 577 pages, written by hand. An Aible is not a replacement for
its subject and does not pretend to be one.

Dates are **Anno Greta**: 2019 is year 0 AG, so 7 AG is 2026. Day and month stay
the same; subtract 2019 from the year.

## What came before #1

Aible #1 is **not** the first Aible ever made. Five came before it, back when the
thing had no name — the word was invented after that book was already finished.
#1 is the first one published *as* an Aible, and the first to carry the definition
inside itself.

Those five are deliberately not attributed. They were not all made with different
machines — at least one AI produced two of them, from different prompting — and the
authorship was never properly recorded. Naming them would be a claim that cannot
be shown, and the sixth of the seven rules forbids exactly that:

> Claim nothing you cannot show.

Leaving them unnamed is the correct answer here, not a gap waiting to be filled.

## Adding your own

1. Make one. The entry rule is the method: there is no time limit writing the prompt
2. Add `aibles/<n>/` containing your documents and a `README.md` giving the author,
   the AI, the date, the method and the licence. If you did not record which AI,
   write **unrecorded** — that is a legitimate value here, not a failure.
3. Open a pull request. Holders of an Identor number may commit directly.

The interesting thing about this register is not any single entry. It is the
comparison across entries: the same material, different machines, different books.
That is a comparison none of the machines can make about itself.
